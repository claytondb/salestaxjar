Before 2018, you only had to collect sales tax in states where you had a physical presence — an office, warehouse, or employees. The *South Dakota v. Wayfair* Supreme Court decision changed everything.

Now every state with a sales tax can require you to collect sales tax based purely on your sales into that state — even if you've never set foot there. This is called **economic nexus**, and it's the most important sales tax concept for online sellers in 2026.

This guide covers every state's current economic nexus threshold, how each state measures it, and whether your marketplace sales count.

## What Is Economic Nexus?

Economic nexus is triggered when your sales into a state pass a threshold, measured by:
- **Dollar amount**: Your sales to customers in that state (every state with a sales tax uses this)
- **Transaction count**: The number of separate sales into that state (only some states still use this)

The 200-transaction test is disappearing. Since 2022, Maine, South Dakota, Louisiana, Indiana, Wyoming, North Carolina, Utah, Illinois and — most recently — Kentucky (August 1, 2026) have dropped it, so in most states only the dollar amount matters now.

## What Counts Toward Your Threshold?

This is where it gets nuanced, and it varies by state:

- **Which sales:** Some states count all your gross sales (taxable and exempt), some count retail sales, and some count only taxable sales. If you sell exempt products — like clothing in Pennsylvania — this can make a real difference.
- **Marketplace sales:** Most states count sales you make through Amazon, Etsy, eBay or Walmart toward your threshold, even though the marketplace collected the tax. About 18 states don't (see the table).
- **Sales for resale:** Wholesale sales to resellers with a valid certificate are usually excluded, but check the state's rule.

**The safest approach:** Count all your sales into a state, then look up how that state treats exempt and marketplace sales. If you're close to a threshold, talk to a tax professional before deciding what to leave out.

## Marketplace Facilitator Laws and Economic Nexus

If you sell through Amazon, Etsy, Walmart Marketplace or similar platforms, the platform collects and remits sales tax on those sales for you. Every state with a sales tax now has a marketplace facilitator law.

But **those sales can still count toward your own economic nexus threshold** — depending on the state.

**Example:** You sell $60,000 directly through your WooCommerce site and $60,000 through Amazon, all shipped to customers in Ohio. Amazon collected tax on the Amazon sales. Ohio counts marketplace sales toward your threshold, so you're at $120,000 — above Ohio's $100,000 threshold — and you may need to register and collect tax on your direct WooCommerce sales.

Ship the same orders to Illinois instead and the answer changes: Illinois doesn't count marketplace sales, so you're at $60,000 — below its threshold.

## When Do You Need to Register?

It depends on the state. Some expect you to register as soon as you pass the threshold, others by the first day of the following month, and states that measure the previous calendar year (like Florida and Pennsylvania) start your obligation on January 1 of the next year.

**Key point:** The obligation begins when you *cross* the threshold, not when you *notice* you crossed it. That's why it pays to check your numbers regularly.

## What Happens If You Don't Register?

If you cross a threshold and don't register, you can be liable for:

1. **Uncollected tax** — You owe the tax you should have collected, even though you didn't collect it
2. **Interest** on the unpaid tax
3. **Penalties** — often a percentage of the tax owed
4. **Back periods** — states can look back several years if you never registered

## State Sales Tax Registration: Quick Overview

When you determine you have economic nexus in a new state:

1. **Apply for a sales tax permit** on the state's department of revenue website. It's free in most states; a few charge a small fee.
2. **Receive your permit number** — some states issue it instantly online, others take a week or two.
3. **Turn on tax collection for that state** in your store.
4. **Note your filing schedule** — the state assigns it (monthly, quarterly or annually), usually based on how much tax you collect.

**Streamlined Sales Tax (SST):** About two dozen states participate in the Streamlined Sales Tax program, which lets you register in all of them at once.

## Key Takeaways

1. **$100,000 in sales is the most common threshold.** California, New York and Texas use $500,000; Alabama and Mississippi use $250,000.
2. **The 200-transaction test is fading.** Only about a third of sales tax states still use it, and Kentucky dropped it in August 2026.
3. **Marketplace sales count in most states** — but not all. Check the table.
4. **Each state measures a different period.** Some look at the calendar year, some at the last 12 months, some only at last year.
5. **Crossing a threshold usually means registering first, then collecting.**
