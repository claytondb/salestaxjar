Sales tax compliance isn't just about collecting the right amount — it's about getting that money to the state on time. Miss a deadline and you're looking at penalties, interest, and a paper trail that could complicate future audits.

The tricky part? Every state sets its own deadlines. Your filing frequency depends on how much sales tax you collect. And the due date isn't always the 20th.

This guide breaks down how filing frequencies work, lists the due dates for every state with a sales tax, and explains what happens if you're late. The dates below were checked against each state's department of revenue in September 2026. Each state assigns your filing frequency, so confirm your schedule with every state where you're registered.

## How Sales Tax Filing Frequency Works

When you register for a sales tax permit, each state assigns you a filing frequency based on your expected or actual sales tax:

- **Monthly** — For sellers with more sales tax to remit. Returns are due on a set day of the following month (often the 20th).
- **Quarterly** — The most common frequency for small sellers. Returns are due the month after each quarter ends.
- **Annual** — For sellers with very little sales tax. One return per year.
- **Semiannual** — A few states (such as Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Hawaii and New Mexico) offer twice-a-year filing to their smallest filers.

States can move you to a more frequent schedule as your sales grow. It's worth checking your account with each state once a year.

> **Important:** Even if you collected zero sales tax in a period, most states still require you to file a "zero return." Skipping it can trigger the same late penalties as if you owed money.

## 2026 Quarterly Due Dates

In most states, a quarterly return is due on the **20th of the month after the quarter**:

| Quarter | Period Covered | Due in most states |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 2026 | January 1 – March 31 | **April 20, 2026** |
| Q2 2026 | April 1 – June 30 | **July 20, 2026** |
| Q3 2026 | July 1 – September 30 | **October 20, 2026** |
| Q4 2026 | October 1 – December 31 | **January 20, 2027** |

But many states don't use the 20th. California, Connecticut, Missouri, North Carolina, North Dakota, Rhode Island, Utah, Washington, Wisconsin and Wyoming want quarterly returns by the **last day** of the month after the quarter (April 30, July 31, October 31, January 31). Kansas, New Mexico and Vermont use the 25th, Maine the 15th, and Massachusetts the 30th.

**New York is different:** its quarters run March–May, June–August, September–November and December–February, so its quarterly returns are due June 20, September 20, December 20 and March 20.

Indiana, Iowa, Ohio and Oklahoma don't offer quarterly filing at all.

## Rules Worth Knowing

**Nevada** changed its due date from the last day of the month to the **20th**, starting with January 2026 returns.

**Massachusetts** returns are due on the **30th**, not the 20th, and have been since 2021.

**Florida** returns are late after the 20th, but electronic payments must be started by 5 p.m. Eastern on the business day *before* the 20th.

**Arizona** treats returns filed and paid electronically as on time through the last business day of the month.

**Michigan** requires every sales tax filer — monthly and quarterly too — to file an annual return by February 28.

**Hawaii** doesn't have an annual-only option, and every filer also files an annual reconciliation (Form G-49), due April 20 for a calendar year.

**Washington** splits its due dates: monthly returns on the 25th, quarterly returns on the last day of the month, and annual returns on April 15.

**California** puts sellers with an average of $17,000 or more of tax a month on quarterly prepayments, with payments due by the 24th of the first two months of each quarter.

**New York** moves you to monthly (part-quarterly) filing if your taxable sales reach $300,000 in a quarter. Its PrompTax program, for vendors with more than about $500,000 of sales tax a year, requires faster electronic payments.

## What Happens If You Miss a Deadline?

Missing a filing deadline — even by a day — can trigger penalties in most states:

- **Late filing or payment penalty:** usually a percentage of the tax due, and often more the longer the return is late
- **Minimum penalties:** some states charge a flat minimum even when little or no tax is due
- **Interest:** charged on unpaid tax until it's paid
- **Estimated assessments:** if you don't file, some states will estimate what you owe and bill you for it, plus penalties — and it's up to you to prove otherwise

**Example:** You owe $400 in Florida sales tax for Q1 and file three weeks late. Florida's penalty is 10% of the tax due with a **$50 minimum**, so you'd owe at least $450, plus interest.

### What to Do If You've Already Missed a Deadline

1. **File as soon as you can** — Penalties and interest keep growing until you file and pay.
2. **Pay what you owe** — Even a partial payment reduces the interest.
3. **Ask about penalty relief** — Many states will waive a first-time penalty if you have a clean history. Look for "penalty waiver" or "abatement" on the state's revenue department website.
4. **Consider voluntary disclosure** — If you have several unfiled periods, many states have voluntary disclosure programs that limit how far back you owe and reduce penalties.

## Key Takeaways

1. **The 20th is the most common due date**, but many states use the last day of the month, and a few use the 15th, 23rd, 25th or 30th.
2. **Quarterly returns are usually due in January, April, July and October** — except New York's, which are due in March, June, September and December.
3. **Indiana, Iowa, Ohio and Oklahoma don't offer quarterly filing.**
4. **Annual returns aren't always due in January** — Michigan's is due February 28, Minnesota's February 5, Washington's April 15, New York's March 20 and DC's October 20.
5. **Zero returns still need to be filed** even if you collected nothing.
6. **Penalties start right away**, and many states have a minimum penalty.
